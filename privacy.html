Privacy policy

Last updated 6 November 2026.

Who’s responsible

PlayCord Limited is responsible for your data under UK data protection law. Contact: support@playcord.app.

What PlayCord keeps

Your account: your email if you save your account (a temporary account has none), your nickname, your country and currency.

Your playlists: each song’s title, artist, album, year, genre, plays, skips and rating. The playlist file itself stays on your phone.

Your collection and plans: records you own, your scores and notes, what you paid and where, your wishlist, equipment, watch list, keystone songs, your answers and your look and feel.

Your month and year of birth, if you give it: to show your age when you’ll get each album, if you switch that on, and to keep Friends, the feed, groups and the message boards to 18 and over. Never shared. “Turn it off”, under your month and year, deletes them; if you were under 18, the month you turn 18 is kept, so those stay closed until then.

A record of each AI lookup and what it cost, to charge your credit and keep within limits.

The day you last opened PlayCord (the date only, not the time or what you did): counted without names, to see how many new members come back.

Friends, if you’re 18 or over and use them: your friends and requests, invite codes, messages, groups and their messages, posts on your feed and comments and reactions on them, and what you choose to let friends see. Posts and comments on the message boards for albums and artists, with your nickname, which every member can see.

Your profile, if you add to it: a photo and a line about you, shown to your friends.

The town you type for record shops near you, if you choose to (it’s optional), and the shops you choose.

Your phone’s notification token, if you allow notifications, so PlayCord can send them.

Broken links you report: the page’s address, the record and shop it was for, and when.

Problems you report: what you write, the screen you were on, PlayCord’s version and your phone’s system and screen size, kept with your account so PlayCord’s team can put them right. If PlayCord itself hits a problem, you can send it with a tap: only the kind of error, where in PlayCord’s code it happened and the screen it was on, never what was on the screen.

How PlayCord runs on your phone, only if you say yes when asked (or turn on “Help make PlayCord faster” in Settings, Your data): how long it takes to open and to show each tab and page, how long the phone was too busy to answer, and any errors in the app, with PlayCord’s version and your phone’s system and screen size. Never your records, notes or name. PlayCord’s staff see it with your nickname, to find and fix what’s slow or broken. Kept 90 days; turning it off deletes it.

Your answers in The Greatest: your top 3s and favourites, and any scores you give them. They count in everyone’s answer, without your name, only if you say yes to sharing your music (asked once, when you start or on Home); you can change that at any time.

Faults you note in a record’s pressing (surface noise, clicks, a warp and the like): counted for everyone without your name, and shown on a record’s page only when at least two members say the same.

Shops and prices from your notes, only if you turn on sharing (beside each note; it starts off): the shop, price, stock and edition of a record you saw at a shop PlayCord lists, and the same facts read by PlayCord’s AI from your notes, all your notes including older ones. Other members see them on the album as “a member saw it”, for 90 days, never with your name or your words, marked proposed until another member, PlayCord’s own check or its staff confirms them; PlayCord never uses them to price anyone’s plan. Your notes’ words aren’t kept by the AI. Turn sharing off and they’re all taken down.

Your gift list, if you make one: the name you put on it, the occasion and its day, and the records on it with their prices, which anyone with its link can see; and the names people leave when they say they’re getting one, kept until two days after the day. You can stop sharing it, or make a new link, at any time.

People getting you a record from your gift list don’t sign up. If they choose to leave their email, PlayCord uses it only to send them two emails: where to buy the record, and a reminder a week before the day. It’s never shown to you or anyone else, every email has a link to stop them, and it’s deleted the day after the day (or after two months for a list with no day), or sooner if they stop the emails or change their mind. To stop anyone using a list to send a stranger email after email, PlayCord counts the emails sent for 30 days, keeping a scrambled form of the address that can’t be read back, never the address itself. If they stop the emails, that scrambled form is kept, so PlayCord never emails them about a gift again, whoever types their address in.

If they also tick the box for occasional emails about PlayCord (it starts unticked), their first email asks them to confirm it, so nobody can sign up someone else. Only once they do does PlayCord keep their email for news, apart from the gift, with when they agreed and the words they agreed to, and every yes and stop since. Every news email has a link to stop them; once stopped, they’re never sent news again unless they ask and confirm again.

What stays on your phone

The photos you choose for the picture behind PlayCord and the screensaver, and album covers once downloaded. They aren’t uploaded.

Why, and the law that allows it

To give you the service you ask for (contract), and to keep it secure and working, fix problems and prevent misuse (legitimate interests). No advertising, no selling your data, and no tracking you across other apps.

PlayCord asks your permission for its AI once, before your first playlist is sent (or before your first piece of AI work, if it wasn’t asked then), and before each piece of AI work you start. Not now turns AI off for your account, so nothing of yours goes to Anthropic; switch it on or off any time in Settings, Membership and AI credit.

Who else handles it

Supabase stores PlayCord’s data and runs its server and sign-in, in London.

Anthropic (Claude AI) receives what each AI answer needs: song, artist and album names to place songs; record names to check prices; the artists you play and the albums you own or plan, for suggestions; artist names for their best-known albums; equipment names, and any photo you send to find equipment; any picture of a receipt, an order or a shop’s page you send, to read what you paid or what a record costs; your notes, if you share them, to pick out the shops and prices in them; an album’s name and its Wikipedia article, for its story.

A Discogs collection file you bring in is read on your phone. MusicBrainz receives song, artist and album names, the albums whose details and pressings you look at, and any catalogue number or barcode you search for under “Which pressing is yours”, the words you search for albums with, the barcodes you scan, the artist names you type in The Greatest, and, if you look for new pressings, the albums in your plan and on your watch list (as MusicBrainz’s ids for them), sent from PlayCord’s server. Wikimedia (Wikidata and Wikipedia) receives the MusicBrainz ids or names of artists whose pages are opened, for their bios, and of albums whose story is asked for, sent from PlayCord’s server; an artist’s photo is fetched by your phone straight from Wikimedia Commons, which sees your phone’s internet address, as any website you visit would.

Apple receives the album and artist names looked up for an album page’s artwork, sent from PlayCord’s server, with your country so the link opens in your own Apple Music store.

Apple takes the payment when you buy a membership or AI credit in the app, and your card details stay with Apple. The app gives Apple your PlayCord account’s id with each purchase, and PlayCord’s server receives Apple’s signed record of it (what was bought, when, and Apple’s number for it), to check it’s genuine and add it to your account.

When you tap Listen (on A song or Now playing), your phone turns up to 15 seconds of the sound around it into a fingerprint and sends only that to Apple’s Shazam, which says which song is playing. The sound itself isn’t recorded, sent or kept; the song found is looked up as one you type would be.

Your phone downloads album covers from the Cover Art Archive (the Internet Archive) and Apple, which see your phone’s internet address, as any website you visit does.

Expo (expo.dev) passes PlayCord’s notifications to your phone, so handles its notification token and each notification’s words.

GitHub hosts the page a gift list opens on, and sees the internet address of whoever opens it, as any website does. The list itself comes from PlayCord’s server; the part of the link that unlocks it isn’t sent to GitHub.

Resend (resend.com) sends PlayCord’s emails, your sign-in codes included, so handles your email address and the email’s words; and to people getting a record from a gift list, when they leave their email.

Shops receive nothing from PlayCord. When you open a shop’s link, you’re on their website, under their terms.

Anthropic, Expo, Resend and GitHub are in the United States. What goes to them is covered by a safeguard UK law recognises: for Anthropic, the UK’s addendum to the EU’s standard contractual clauses, in Anthropic’s data processing terms; for Expo, Resend and GitHub, the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (the UK-US data bridge), which each is certified to. Write to us for a copy of any of these.

How long

While your account exists. A temporary account not used for 30 days is deleted with everything in it. Deleting your account deletes your data at once, the messages and posts you wrote included. Records of what you bought through the App Store are kept for the accounts and tax, no longer linked to you; details about records (covers, prices, which album a song is on) aren’t about you, so they’re kept for everyone, and so is what PlayCord’s AI cost, without who it was for.

Your rights

You can get a copy of your data (Settings, Download my data), correct it, delete it (Settings, Delete my account), and object to or ask us to limit how we use it. If you’re unhappy with how we handle it, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk).

Under 18s

PlayCord is for people aged 13 and over. It has no advertising or profiling, and the most private settings are the default.

Security

Connections are encrypted, each member can read only their own data, and signing in uses an emailed code rather than a password.

Changes

We update this policy as PlayCord changes. The date at the top is the latest version’s, and we’ll tell you in the app before any important change to how we use your data.

Questions about any of this: support@playcord.app

How it works

From playlist to plan in three steps.

01

Bring your playlist

Import your Apple Music or Spotify playlists, a CSV file, or paste a list. You see what’s in it straight away: the artists, and the albums your songs point to.

02

Tell it how you buy

A few questions: what you can spend a month, new, used or originals, which kinds of record count, and the songs that must be in. Change any of it whenever you like.

03

Get your plan

Every album you need, in the order to buy them, month by month, each with where it’s cheapest. When something changes, the plan changes with it.

Buying

It never passes off a guess as a price.

Prices come off each shop’s own page, for that album on vinyl, with delivery counted in. Before a shop is checked, an album shows a first guess: a range, clearly marked, that the shop’s price replaces. A page that’s gone is dropped before you ever see it.

Every seller’s delivery, found for you

Read off each shop’s own delivery page. Where a shop only shows it at checkout, PlayCord says so rather than make a figure up.

Multi-buys, worked out

A shop’s 3 for £66 is summed against buying each record on its own, and suggested only when it really saves. One record short? It shows which would complete the set.

Shops abroad, when it’s not here

Out of stock at home, or dear at home? It looks further afield, with the price in pounds and the delivery to you.

A plan that bends

Put a little aside each month for Record Store Day or Black Friday. Skip a month and its money carries on. Choose which month a purchase comes out of.

Your shelf

Rated twice: the music, and the pressing.

Score the album and the pressing separately. Note surface noise, clicks, a warp or a skip, and keep which pressing yours is, its grades and what you paid. Ranked puts your favourites in order.

New to records?

Start with the songs you love. Learn the rest as you go.

Not sure what a pressing is, or what VG+ means? PlayCord explains as it goes, and starts you with the records that hold the most of what you play.

What a pressing is

One run of a record: one label, one country, one year. The label and the catalogue number on the sleeve say which you have.

Originals and reissues

An original was pressed when the album first came out; a reissue later, often remastered. New copies of older albums are reissues, and many sound excellent.

What the grades mean

Used copies are graded for the record and the sleeve, from Mint to Poor. Very Good Plus and better plays well; from Very Good down, expect some noise.

A budget that holds

Say what you spend a month. PlayCord fills each month with the records that hold the most of your songs, with delivery counted, and keeps within it.

AI credit

What it buys, and why you pay for it.

PlayCord uses Anthropic’s Claude AI for the work that needs judgement. You pay only for what it does, from credit, and you choose how much it may do.

Why does PlayCord use AI at all?

A playlist says where you heard a song, not which record to buy: often a compilation, a single, a remaster or a best-of. Matching thousands of songs to the albums you’d want on vinyl, and reading shop pages for today’s prices and stock, takes judgement at a speed no person could manage. PlayCord settles all it can without AI first, from your playlist’s own albums and MusicBrainz, so the AI only works on what’s left.

What does credit buy?

About 0.2p to place a song your playlist doesn’t settle, or 2p for a song you search for. About 9p to search the shops for a record, and about 0.6p to keep its price fresh. About 0.1p for a first guess at an album’s price. Under 1p for suggestions, and 1p to 5p for other lookups, such as your equipment or shops near you. Ask PlayCord: 7 questions free a month, then 5p.

So £1.00 places about 475 songs, or checks about 11 records at the shops.

Why credit, rather than a dearer membership?

Every piece of AI work costs to run, and people use very different amounts: one playlist has 200 songs, another 10,000. With credit you pay for what you use and no more, the membership stays at £3.99 a month, and nobody pays for anyone else’s.

What’s free?

Save your account with an email address and you get 20p of credit to try the AI. Members get £1.00 every month, and their first songs are placed at PlayCord’s cost. Songs your playlist settles itself, and records someone checked in the last three days, cost nothing. When an answer fails, or the AI isn’t sure of it, PlayCord usually asks again on a stronger model at its own cost.

What happens when it runs out?

The AI work stops: you’re never charged more than your credit. Everything else carries on, your plan, your shelf and the prices already found. Members get £1.00 more each month, and can top up with a pack whenever they like.

Can I control what it spends?

Turn any kind of AI work off, set the most it may spend in a month (from £1 to £100), and be warned when your credit falls below 50p, £1 or £2. Every piece of work is listed with what it cost, and credit you don’t use is kept.

What does the AI see?

Only what each answer needs: song, artist and album names to place songs; record names to check prices; the artists you play and the albums you own or plan, for suggestions; and any photo you send to find your equipment.

What if it gets something wrong?

It can. Each song says how its album was found and how sure PlayCord is, and “Wrong album?” on a song puts it right; what you say is kept. Each price says which shop and when it was checked.

About PlayCord

Which playlists can it read?

Apple Music (a playlist exported from the Music app on a computer), Spotify (the copy of your data Spotify sends you) and any CSV with the song, the artist and the album, such as one from Exportify. Your Discogs collection file too.

Does PlayCord sell records?

No. It finds the best price with delivery at the shops and takes you there to buy. Your budget is a plan, not a payment: PlayCord never spends your money on records.

Which countries?

The UK first: prices in pounds, from UK shops and from shops abroad that deliver to the UK.

Which devices?

iPhone and iPad first, the iPhone Duo open or closed too; Android to follow. Your plan, your shelf and your look follow your account to each one.

Membership and your data

Can I try it first?

Yes, free and with no sign-up: your plan, your shelf and prices. Save your account with an email address to try the AI, with 20p of credit.

How do I cancel?

In the App Store, from your iPhone’s Settings. Only going away for a while? Members can take a holiday: PlayCord stops checking anything by itself, so none of your credit is spent.

What does PlayCord keep?

Your playlists’ songs, your plan, shelf, kit and settings, so they’re on each of your devices. Your own photos stay on your phone.

Can I download or delete my data?

Yes, both from Settings: Download my data saves all of it as a file, and Delete my account deletes everything.
